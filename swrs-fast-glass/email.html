Nicole — thanks for reaching out about Fast Glass’s receivables.

You asked four specific things — fee structure, account minimums, collection process, and how we handle consumer and commercial accounts side by side — so let me answer them straight rather than send you a brochure. I’ve also put together a capabilities overview built around a glass and glazing book specifically; the link is further down.

Fees. Pure contingency. No upfront cost, no retainer, no monthly fee, and nothing at all on what we don’t collect. You pay a pre-agreed percentage only of what we actually recover. The rate scales with the age, balance size and volume of what you place, so I’ll quote you a real number once I’ve seen a representative sample of your aging rather than guessing at it in an email.

Minimums. None — no minimum balance per account, no minimum placement volume. A $600 residential glass balance is worth placing alongside a $22,000 commercial glazing invoice.

The process — and it isn’t just phone calls. Every account is scored first, which sets the contact strategy before anyone reaches out. From there it’s compliant outreach across text, email, live and AI-assisted calls, voicemail drops and mailed notices, plus a secure 24/7 client portal where you see status on every account in real time.

The workflows run in real time rather than on a fixed schedule. No reply to an email auto-triggers a text. A missed call triggers a personalized voicemail plus a follow-up email. Still nothing, and the account moves to skip tracing or legal review. Contact lands when that particular debtor actually engages, which produces more right-party contacts, faster settlements and higher recovery than a standing call list.

Consumer and commercial together. Both, under one agreement, routed automatically by debtor type — you don’t sort them. Your homeowner balances run the consumer track under the FDCPA and Reg F: validation notice, call-frequency limits, cease-communication honored, every step documented and auditable. Your contractor, property-manager and commercial invoices run the B2B trade track, which sits outside the FDCPA and allows direct engagement with AP, the controller and ownership. Credit reporting, where lawful and appropriate, is to Equifax and Experian. We’ve collected millions of dollars in industries just like yours since 2004.

One thing worth flagging before you place anything: we don’t work consumer accounts where the debtor resides in California, Oregon or Washington. Those are returned at intake. Denver metro, that’s unlikely to touch you, but you should know it going in.

What we’d need from you. For each account: the invoice or work order, the signed estimate or contract, any change orders, last known contact details for the customer, a running balance with payments applied, and any photos or correspondence if the job was ever disputed. On the commercial side, the legal entity name and address if it differs from the name on the invoice. All of it uploads straight into the portal from your existing aging export — we map your columns, so nothing gets re-keyed.

Since you mentioned wanting this built into your AR process long-term rather than run as a one-off: that’s the version that actually pays. Recovery drops steeply with age, so a standing trigger — everything at 90 days past due, automatically — recovers materially more than cleaning out the aging once a year. On contingency there’s no cost to placing early. I’d also mention we handle full outsourced AR management and insurance follow-up if either becomes useful as you grow.

I’ll be straight about the accounts you already have aged: those sit at the lower end of the range in the overview, not the top. The case studies in it run from 49% down to 15% recovered, and an aged book behaves like the bottom half of that. The real value here is what you place next year, on a trigger.
