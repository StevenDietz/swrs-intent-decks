Bryan — thank you for reaching out. I’m the founder, and I’ll handle this directly rather than route you into a business development queue.

Before the mechanics, the thing I think actually matters here: a delinquent Club Plan account belongs to someone who still lives in the community and whose neighbours will be at the Lagoon on Saturday. You want that household back in good standing, not gone. Everything below is built around that, because recovery that costs you a resident is not recovery.

You listed your requirements in order, so I’ll answer them in your order — including the one where the honest answer has a condition attached.

Fee structure. Pure contingency, and it is the only model we have run since 2004. No upfront cost, no setup fee, no retainer, no monthly minimum, nothing per account. You pay a pre-agreed percentage only of what we actually recover. The rate is driven by the age, average balance and volume of what you place, and I quote you a real number once I have seen a representative sample of your delinquent Club Plan aging — not a range on a website.

Phone, email, text and mail. All four, sequenced by what each account actually responds to rather than by volume. Every account is scored first to set the contact strategy, then worked with compliant outreach across text, email, live and AI-assisted calls, voicemail drops and mailed notices, with a secure 24/7 portal behind it. One note on text specifically: SMS requires documented prior express consent, so I will want to see the consent language in your Club Plan enrollment before a single message goes out. Where it isn’t there, we work email, phone and mail and we tell you rather than assuming.

Payment arrangements. Set up and administered end to end. Our collectors negotiate inside limits you define — minimum payment, maximum term, settlement authority — and we hold to whatever floor you set. Payments post to your portal in real time, and an arrangement that breaks is re-engaged automatically instead of going quiet.

Disputed accounts. A dispute pauses recovery; it does not end it. We log it, send written validation, and work it against the paper — the Club Plan agreement, the billing and payment history, the communication trail. On a dues book the agreement usually settles it quickly. Disputes are documented account by account and reported back, so you see the pattern as well as the outcome.

Skip tracing. Standard on every placement, never an add-on. Address, phone and employment tracing through national databases, plus property-record work that is unusually effective in your case: the obligation follows a home you can already locate, which makes a Club Plan account materially easier to work than most consumer balances.

Reporting and escalation. Inside the portal you get live account status, recovery-to-date and remittance detail on demand, by community or in aggregate, plus direct-payment logging so a member who pays MetroLagoons directly stops being contacted the same day. Where normal recovery is exhausted we review the account for credit reporting with Equifax and Experian and, with your written authorisation account by account, legal escalation through our attorney network.

Vantaca. You don’t need an integration to start. Place accounts by uploading the delinquency export Vantaca already produces — we map your columns to ours, so nothing is re-keyed. If volume justifies it later, we move to scheduled SFTP or API placement so accounts start working without the data lag.

Post-chargeback guest balances — yes, with a condition. Once an issuer decides a dispute for the cardholder, the card network remedy is finished, but the underlying obligation between MetroLagoons and that guest normally survives it. We can pursue it where you can produce the transaction record, the terms the guest accepted at purchase, and the issuer’s final decision. Where that documentation is thin, recovery runs materially below what your dues book will do — so I would scope, price and report chargebacks as their own portfolio rather than blend them into the dues numbers and flatter both.

One more honest point, because it will come up on the call. Dues books vary more by age at placement than by anything else. Accounts placed inside roughly 90 to 120 days of delinquency recover several times what the same accounts return after a year of internal follow-up, because the balance has not yet compounded past what a household believes it can pay. The case studies in the overview show that spread openly, low numbers included. We’ve collected millions of dollars in industries just like yours since 2004.
