01 — Introduction

Recovery, not billing — the layer that starts where your claim ends

Prepared for Bloom Behavioral Health — a dual-certified psychiatric and family nurse practitioner practice delivering holistic mental health care to Louisiana patients from school age through the golden years, primarily by telehealth with limited in-person days in New Orleans. A practice built for flexibility carries a specific receivable: patient responsibility that accrues quietly across recurring medication-management and psychotherapy visits, and payer balances that stall in follow-up long after the visit was delivered. Southwest Recovery Services is not a billing company and does not replace your revenue-cycle workflow — we are the post-billing recovery layer that works what your process could not close.

Care already delivered is revenue already earned. In behavioral health the balance is rarely large, but it is recurring — a coinsurance amount on every medication-management visit, a deductible that resets each January, a no-show or late-cancellation fee, a self-pay balance from a patient between plans. Individually each is small enough to ignore. Across a growing panel they become the difference between a practice that scales and one that quietly finances its own patients.

Southwest Recovery Services works healthcare receivables on pure contingency since 2004 — under a Business Associate Agreement, with HIPAA and Regulation F training for every account handled, and credit reporting only where lawful. No retainer, no minimum, no recovery, no fee.

03 — Your questions, answered

What a practice owner asks us — answered directly

The questions that matter when the receivable is patient responsibility on recurring behavioral-health visits, and the practice owner is also the clinician.

Your questionSouthwest Recovery Services
What does it cost?Pure contingency — $0 upfront, no retainer, no monthly minimum. You pay a pre-agreed percentage only of what we actually recover. Nothing about placing accounts adds a fixed cost to a practice that is still growing.
Is this billing, or something else?Something else. We do not code, bill, or submit claims, and we do not replace your revenue-cycle workflow. We are the layer that starts after billing has done its job — aged patient balances, self-pay accounts, and payer receivables that stalled in follow-up. Think of it as the backstop behind your process, not a substitute for it.
How is patient privacy handled?Every healthcare client is worked under a Business Associate Agreement. Staff carry ongoing HIPAA and Regulation F training, and we operate on the minimum-necessary standard — we work a balance without ever needing a clinical record. For behavioral health specifically, the sensitivity of the underlying care is exactly why the recovery has to be quiet, professional and documented.
Will this damage the therapeutic relationship?That is the right question to ask, and it drives how we work the account. Recovery is firm, professional, and never threatening or misrepresenting — no pressure tactics, no implied consequences that don’t exist. A patient who resolves a balance respectfully can and often does stay in care. That protects your panel and your reputation in a community where reputation is the referral engine.
What about Medicaid and Medicare patients?Those balances are governed differently, and we work only what is lawfully collectible. Medicaid cost-sharing is limited or prohibited across most eligibility categories, so there is often little to no patient responsibility to pursue. Medicare Part B coinsurance is collectible patient responsibility. We screen the file with you before anything is worked, rather than pursuing balances that shouldn’t be.
What does placement look like for a small practice?Low-lift. Upload an aging export through the secure portal — we map your columns, so nothing is re-keyed — and outreach begins within 24 to 48 hours of a documented placement. You log any payment a patient makes directly to the practice so balances stay accurate and no one is over-contacted.

Why early-out matters

Patient balances are most collectible while care is recent

A patient balance is most collectible while the visit is still recent and the relationship is still warm; every month it ages, contact information goes stale and willingness to resolve drops. For a recurring-visit practice, the highest-leverage change is rarely chasing harder — it is placing sooner.

The age curve

Balances placed within 90–120 days of becoming patient responsibility recover materially better than balances that sit a year.

The standing rule

A fixed placement policy beats case-by-case decisions, and on contingency there is no cost to placing.

Recovery figures vary by portfolio and payer mix; SWRS gives an honest, age-based read on a representative sample before any accounts are worked.
