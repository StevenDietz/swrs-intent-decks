Thank you for reaching out about help with Urban Strategy’s outstanding invoices. You mentioned roughly $500,000 outstanding, so I put together a short overview built around a book that size and around how engineering receivables actually behave.

The structural problem first. Your work is front-loaded. The grading and drainage design, the utility layout, the ALTA and boundary surveys — those land early and cost you payroll and licensed crews. The developer’s money arrives late, if the project clears at all. So an aging engineering invoice usually is not a refusal. It is a project waiting on financing, on entitlements, or on somebody else’s capital partner.

The part that makes land development different: a lot of that work is billed to single-purpose entities. The project LLC goes quiet, the balance looks uncollectible, and it gets written off. But the principals, the parent developer and the successor entity are usually findable. We run commercial skip tracing on the entity, its registered agent, its officers and its affiliates, so we are working the party that actually has money rather than the shell that signed the contract.

On scope disputes, which are the norm in engineering: a dispute pauses recovery, it does not end it. We validate against the paper — executed proposal, change orders, submitted deliverables, the plan-review and revision trail. In your work the deliverable is usually decisive, because the stamped plans were issued and the survey was recorded.

One thing worth separating out. Work billed to a developer, to a prime contractor, and to a public agency behave nothing alike. Agency payables move slowly but reliably and need process pressure, not legal pressure. Balances behind a prime need the chain documented. We work those as three approaches rather than running one script across the whole book, and it changes what a realistic recovery number looks like for each slice.

On cost: pure contingency. $0 upfront, no retainer, no monthly minimum. You pay a pre-agreed percentage only of what we actually recover, so there is no risk in placing and no cost if we do not collect. We quote your rate once we have seen a representative sample of your aging.

Something I would rather raise now than after we start. On construction and development balances there are often statutory remedies with hard filing deadlines attached. Those are a matter for your counsel, not for us, but if nobody has reviewed them against the oldest invoices in this book, that is worth doing in parallel with placement rather than after. Waiting can cost you options that recovery work cannot get back.

What we would need: an AR-aging export from QuickBooks, Deltek, Ajera or a spreadsheet. We map your columns, so there is no re-keying and no new workflow for your accounting team. Work begins within 24 to 48 hours of a documented placement.

We have collected millions of dollars in industries just like yours since 2004, and our recovery reach covers all 50 states — which lines up with your licensure across Texas, Oklahoma, Louisiana, Arizona and Colorado. If it is useful, we also handle outsourced AR management, so balances get worked well before they ever reach third-party placement.
