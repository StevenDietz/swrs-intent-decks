01 — Introduction

When another business owes you money, the rules are different

Thank you for reaching out. If your business is owed money by another business, you need a commercial recovery partner — not a consumer collector. B2B debt follows different rules, moves through different contacts, and needs a different approach entirely.

Commercial receivables are not consumer debt. There is no FDCPA regulation on B2B accounts, no 7-in-7 call limits, no mini-Miranda. The contacts are corporate — AP departments, controllers, owners — and the negotiation requires people who understand how businesses actually operate. Our agents are trained specifically for business-to-business work, not retasked from consumer recovery.

Southwest Recovery Services has worked commercial accounts on pure contingency since 2004, from 12 offices across Texas, Colorado, Oklahoma, Missouri, Ohio, Florida and Georgia, with recovery reach nationwide. You place accounts through a secure portal that takes your AR aging export — we map your columns, so nothing gets re-keyed. No recovery, no fee.

03 — Your questions, answered

The questions every business asks first

Answered directly, before you spend time on a call.

Your questionSouthwest Recovery Services
1. What it costsPure contingency — $0 upfront, no retainer, no monthly minimum. You pay a pre-agreed percentage only of what we actually recover. Commercial rates generally run 20–35% depending on the age, balance and volume of what you place. We quote your rate once we have seen a representative sample of your receivables, not before.
2. How fast it moves — and why timing decides the outcomeWe begin working accounts within one business day of placement, running skip-trace verification and contact research before the first outreach. Accounts placed within 60–90 days of the due date typically see first contact in 24–48 hours and resolution in 30–60 days. Older accounts, or those needing skip tracing, run 90–180 days. The single highest-leverage change most finance teams make is not chasing harder — it is placing sooner.
3. What happens to the business relationshipB2B collections done badly ends partnerships. Done properly it resolves the balance and often preserves the relationship. We are direct, professional and persistent — no harassment, no threats, nothing that reflects badly on you. We offer payment-plan structures for businesses that are genuinely struggling rather than simply avoiding, and we can work under a first-party model that keeps our involvement invisible to your customer entirely.
4. Disputed invoicesA dispute pauses recovery; it does not end it. We document the original agreement, the payment history and every communication your team has already had, then present the facts and negotiate toward resolution. Purchase orders, freight charges, returned goods, sales tax and contract terms are the usual sticking points. If the dispute has merit we tell you. If it does not, we press forward.
5. Commercial credit reporting and legal escalationEligible commercial accounts can be reported to the business credit bureaus — Dun & Bradstreet, Experian Business and Equifax Business — which creates real motivation for a debtor that depends on business credit for financing or vendor terms. If negotiation does not move the account we evaluate legal viability and bring you a recommendation: pre-legal demand, litigation, judgment enforcement, asset recovery. We do not push accounts to litigation automatically. You decide.

The age curve

Why fresh commercial invoices recover best

A commercial balance is most collectible while it is fresh. Every month it ages, contact data goes stale, the debtor re-budgets around it, and leverage erodes. Most businesses work an account internally for 90 or 120 days first — by the time it reaches us the easy recovery window has already closed.

What the spread above reflects

The books that recovered 60–78% arrived with strong documentation and reasonable age. The books at 15–21% were large, aged portfolios placed after long internal effort.

The standing rule

A fixed “place at 90 days” policy recovers materially more than letting balances drift toward write-off — and on contingency there is no cost to placing sooner.

Recovery varies by portfolio, documentation quality and aging mix. Before any accounts are worked we give you an honest, age-based read on a representative sample — including where we think recovery will be thin.
