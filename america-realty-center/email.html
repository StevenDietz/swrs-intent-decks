Thank you for reaching out about the former-tenant balances at America Realty Center. You asked eight specific questions, so I have answered all eight below in the order you asked them, and put a short overview together around a residential rental book carrying post-eviction judgments.

1. Single-family residential rentals. Yes — it is a core part of our consumer book. Unpaid rent, late fees, lease-break and early-termination charges, damage beyond the deposit, billed-back utilities and post-eviction deficiency balances are all placeable. We work single-family managers, small residential portfolios, multifamily operators and HOAs, and we are licensed to work Georgia accounts.

2. Contingency rate. Pure contingency — a pre-agreed percentage of what we actually recover, and nothing on an account that does not collect. The percentage moves with the age of the balance, the size of the account and the annual volume you place. I will quote your rate once I have seen a representative sample of your aging. Judgment accounts and accounts another agency has already worked price differently from fresh move-outs, which is why I would rather look first than guess at a number that changes later.

3. Upfront, setup or placement fees. None. No upfront cost, no retainer, no monthly minimum, no per-account placement fee, no charge to open the relationship, and no charge for accounts we fail to collect. The only item that ever carries a separate one-time cost is optional enrollment in consumer credit reporting, and that is disclosed in writing with the agreement before you commit to it.

4. Skip tracing and interstate collection. Both, and on this file they matter more than anything else. Skip tracing runs on the Experian data stack — address, phone, employment and asset location on a resident who moved without notice. Recovery reach is nationwide across all 50 states, so a former tenant who left Georgia stays workable. There is no separate skip-tracing charge; it is inside the contingency.

5. Credit bureau reporting. Yes, for eligible accounts. We report to Equifax and Experian, and for rental debt specifically we also report to Experian RentBureau — the rental-payment database that property managers and screening companies pull when a former resident applies for their next lease. On residential rental balances that is frequently the strongest single point of leverage you have, because it surfaces at the exact moment the person needs something from a landlord. Reporting is governed by FCRA: accounts get validated, disputes investigated, and balances updated when they change.

6. Judgments and legal enforcement. Yes, and I want to be precise about the structure. Southwest Recovery Services is a receivables recovery partner, not your law firm. We pursue a judgment debtor directly through skip tracing, demand and negotiation. Where enforcement requires court action — garnishment, levy, or domesticating a Georgia judgment into another state — the account is referred to licensed counsel in our legal-forwarding network, only on your written authorization, with the cost structure and likely timeline given to you before anything is filed. A judgment that has simply gone unenforced is often still very collectible.

7. Settlement authority. You keep it. Settlements and reduced payoffs are governed by whatever authority you grant in the agreement. Many clients pre-authorize a standing discount range so routine settlements can close on the phone without waiting; anything outside that range comes back to you in writing. If you would rather sign off on every settlement individually, we set it up that way.

8. Documents to place an account. For a standard rental balance: the signed lease, the final ledger or move-out statement showing how the balance was built, the itemized deposit disposition, the resident’s last known address, and any phone or email on file. Date of birth and Social Security number, where your application captured them, materially improve both skip tracing and credit reporting. For the judgment account, add the certified judgment or court order, the case number and county, and any record of post-judgment payments. Thin documentation does not block a placement, but it changes what we can defend if the former resident disputes.

One honest thing before the call. Former-tenant debt is among the harder consumer balances to recover, and I would rather you hear the real range from me than a flattering one. Across our residential rental book, results run from roughly 3% on large bulk portfolios of aged balances with thin paperwork up to the 60s on small, well-documented files placed while the trail is still warm. A single $20,000 account with a certified judgment, a lease and a ledger behind it sits at the strong end of that range, not the weak end.
