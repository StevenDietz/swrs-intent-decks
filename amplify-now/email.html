Rhys,

Thank you for reaching out. You asked two straight questions — what it costs and how the relationship works — so I’ll answer both directly rather than send you a brochure.

On pricing, we work on pure contingency. There is no upfront fee, no retainer, no monthly minimum and no per-account charge; you pay a pre-agreed percentage only of what we actually recover. I’m deliberately not quoting you a single number in this email, because commercial contingency is priced off the age, the balance size and the volume of what you place. A handful of large, recent enterprise invoices prices very differently from a long tail of small aged balances, and a rate quoted before I’ve seen your aging is a rate that turns out to be wrong. Send me a representative sample of your U.S. accounts and I’ll put a real number against it.

On how it works, it is lighter than most finance teams expect. A signed contingency agreement and a placement file is the entire setup — there is no software to buy, nothing to implement, and nothing for your team to operate. You upload an aging or AR export through our secure portal and we map your columns, so nothing gets re-keyed. We work the accounts, and we remit monthly with a transmittal that reconciles line by line to your ledger. You keep live visibility on status and recovery throughout rather than waiting for a monthly PDF. You can place a single account or an entire aging bucket, there’s no volume commitment, and you can stop at any time.

Two things I’d flag before you go further, because they’ll shape whether we’re the right fit. The first is geography. Our recovery reach is nationwide across all fifty U.S. states, but Amplify runs out of Adelaide with teams in Los Angeles, Austin, Toronto, London and Mumbai, so the question that matters is where each paying entity actually sits. We’d scope your U.S.-domiciled debtors first and handle Australian, Canadian and UK balances case by case through our legal-forwarding network. Tell me where the invoices sit and I’ll tell you plainly which ones we should and shouldn’t take.

The second is how your customers get handled. Looking at who you work with, the accounts in question are enterprises you very likely still want a renewal conversation with, and that changes the job. A commercial placement is worked as a documented dispute between two businesses — order form, MSA, statement of work, acceptance, the email trail — not as a consumer collection. In my experience most silent enterprise invoices aren’t a refusal to pay at all; the sponsor left, the PO closed, or the entity was restructured, and finding whoever now owns the payable resolves a real share of them on first contact. Every contact is logged and you can read the account activity yourself in the portal whenever you want. We’ve collected millions of dollars in industries just like yours since 2004.
