01 — Introduction

Third-party recovery for the balances your settlement desk has already worked

Prepared for Trekker Logistics LLC — a privately held motor carrier and freight brokerage founded in 1978, headquartered on Bunkum Road in Caseyville, Illinois with operations in Arkansas, Colorado, Iowa and Texas, running Freightliner Cascadia tractors and 53-foot dry vans across the 48 contiguous states and most of Canada. A carrier that contracts owner-operators produces a receivable most businesses never see: settlement deficits that survive the final settlement, fuel and toll advances drawn against future loads, escrow shortfalls, equipment and lease-purchase obligations, damage and insurance chargebacks, and contractual termination fees — all owed by a contractor who has already turned in the truck. On the brokerage side, the exposure runs the other direction: shipper invoices that age past terms while the carrier has already been paid.

Freight already hauled is revenue already earned. A post-termination balance is rarely disputed on the merits — the contractor simply leaves. A driver runs a negative settlement into the final week and hands back the keys. Advances drawn in October are still outstanding in March. A lease-purchase unit comes back with a deficiency balance and damage chargebacks. Escrow gets returned and the deficit stays. Your settlement desk chases these between payroll cycles for as long as it can, and then the account sits.

Southwest Recovery Services is the third-party layer that starts there. We work commercial and business-purpose receivables on pure contingency since 2004 — nationwide, with FDCPA, Regulation F and FCRA training on every account. No retainer, no minimum, no recovery, no fee.

03 — Your questions, answered

What a carrier’s settlement and AR desk asks us — answered directly

The questions that matter when the receivable is a departed contractor’s settlement deficit on one side of the house and an aged shipper invoice on the other.

Your questionSouthwest Recovery Services
Do you actually work owner-operator and independent-contractor balances?Yes, routinely, and on the commercial track. The obligation arises out of a business contract for business purposes, so it is business-purpose debt, not consumer debt — that governs how it is worked. Settlement deficits, fuel and toll advances, escrow shortfalls, equipment and lease-purchase deficiency balances, damage and insurance chargebacks, unreturned equipment, and contractual termination fees are all in scope.
What does third-party recovery cost?Pure contingency — $0 upfront, no retainer, no monthly minimum. You pay a pre-agreed percentage only of what we actually recover. The rate scales with the age, balance size and volume of what you place; we quote it once we see a representative sample of your file.
What has to be in the placement file?This is what decides the outcome. Per account we need the signed independent-contractor operating agreement and equipment lease, the settlement history showing how the deficit accrued, the advance ledger with authorizations, the escrow ledger and its final accounting, the termination notice and date, the contractor’s entity name, and last known contact. Complete files perform. Thin files convert to disputes.
Will 49 CFR Part 376 come up?Frequently, and you should expect it. Part 376.12 requires every chargeback item and its method of computation to be specified in the lease, and requires a written escrow accounting and return within 45 days of termination. A contractor who wants to contest a deficit reaches for that first. Where your lease is specific and the escrow accounting was issued, these accounts hold up. Where it was not, we will tell you before we work the file rather than after.
What about the brokerage side?Worked separately, as straight commercial B2B recovery. Unpaid shipper invoices, short-pays, deducted claims and aged trade balances are validated against the rate confirmation, bill of lading and proof of delivery, and pursued against the business. In most carrier-plus-broker operations this is the larger and faster-moving of the two books.
How is the contractor contacted?Firm, professional, documented — and never in a way that costs you re-hires or a reputation in a small driver market. Most of these people are still driving somewhere, and drivers talk. Our process is built around preserving your standing in the recruiting pool while still collecting the balance.
How does placement work?Upload an export from your TMS or settlement system through our secure portal — we map your columns, so nothing gets re-keyed. Outreach begins within 24–48 hours of a documented placement, and you can pull reports and log direct payments yourself at any time.

Why early placement matters

A contractor balance is most collectible while the driver is still findable

Post-termination balances decay faster than most because the person owing the money has already left and is usually driving for someone else. Every month after termination, phone numbers change, the entity dissolves, and the balance drifts toward write-off.

The age curve

Balances placed within 90–120 days of the final settlement recover materially better than balances that sit a year past termination.

The standing rule

A fixed placement policy beats case-by-case decisions, and on contingency there is no cost to placing.

Recovery figures vary by portfolio, documentation quality and lease terms; SWRS gives an honest, age-based read on a representative sample before any accounts are worked.
