On what recovery actually looks like, I'd rather give you the real spread than a flattering number. Across comparable trade and water-service books we've worked, results run from about 59% down to roughly 8% of the balance placed. The high end is a well-documented file placed promptly — a regional well contractor placed about $141,000 with us and we returned $83,000 of it. The low end is an aged book with thin paperwork: another contractor placed about $124,000 that had been sitting, and we recovered $10,000. Between those, HVAC at 49%, irrigation and landscaping at 35%, high-volume residential plumbing at 23%. The difference is almost entirely age and documentation, not effort. If most of your $200,000 is 90 days or older — and on a service book it usually is — you should expect the lower half of that range, not the top of it. Balances two years out with a phone number that no longer works are the hardest dollars in the file, and I'd tell you that on the call rather than after you'd signed something.

A couple of things specific to your work are worth naming now. Ozark karst means two wells a hundred feet apart can produce very differently, so "you didn't find enough water" is a predictable objection on a job you performed correctly — we work that off the signed work authorization, the drilling log, the depth and yield records and the DNR well certification, and where the paper is clean the objection usually collapses. Your builder and general-contractor accounts behave differently again: the GC is often paid in full while your invoice waits, so those get pursued against the operating entity, its successors and its principals. Farm and irrigation balances we work around the season instead of against it.

The paperwork is lighter than most people expect.
