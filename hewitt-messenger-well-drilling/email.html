Before I get to rates I want to answer the question I'd be asking in your position, which is how the people who owe you get treated once an account leaves your office. Since most of your balances are owed by individual homeowners, that matters more than the percentage. Hewitt-Messenger is sitting at 4.5 stars across 356 reviews in a market where people talk, and one bad phone call to somebody in Nixa costs more than the invoice it was chasing. Every contact we make is compliant, documented and recorded — no threats, no misrepresentation, no calls to neighbors or employers. In practice most people behind on a well invoice aren't refusing to pay; they're waiting on an insurance check, a closing, or a paycheck. Approached that way a meaningful share of them pay, and they stay your customer for the next pump.

On what recovery actually looks like, I'd rather give you the real spread than a flattering number. Across comparable trade and water-service books we've worked, results run from about 59% down to roughly 8% of the balance placed. The high end is a well-documented file placed promptly — a regional well contractor placed about $141,000 with us and we returned $83,000 of it. The low end is an aged book with thin paperwork: another contractor placed about $124,000 that had been sitting a long while, and we recovered $10,000. In between, HVAC at 49%, irrigation and landscaping at 35%, high-volume residential plumbing at 23%. The difference is almost entirely age and documentation, not effort. If most of your $200,000 is 90 days or older — and on a service book it usually is — plan on the lower half of that range rather than the top. Balances two years out, where the homeowner has moved and the phone number is dead, are the hardest dollars in the file. I'd rather say that now than after you'd signed something.

Two things specific to your work are worth naming. Ozark karst means two wells a hundred feet apart can produce very differently, so "you didn't find enough water" is a predictable objection on a job you performed correctly — we work that off the signed work authorization, the drilling log, the depth and yield records and the DNR well certification, and where the paper is clean the objection usually collapses. And your builder and general-contractor accounts are the minority of the file but the largest individual balances; the GC is often paid in full while your invoice waits, so those get pursued against the operating entity, its successors and its principals rather than chased as ordinary past-due.

The paperwork is lighter than most people expect. There's no setup fee and no monthly minimum — it's pure contingency, so you pay a pre-agreed percentage only of what we actually recover, and nothing at all if we recover nothing. To place accounts you export your aging out of QuickBooks, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro or whatever you're running, or just hand us a spreadsheet, and we map your columns so nothing gets re-keyed on your end. Outreach starts within 24 to 48 hours and you can see every account in the portal from there.

One more thing worth flagging given the Dallas plans: our home office is in Addison, about twenty minutes north of downtown Dallas, and Texas is one of the seven states we operate in. As you open that market you'd be placing Texas accounts with a partner already licensed and staffed there, and you and I would be in the same time zone.
