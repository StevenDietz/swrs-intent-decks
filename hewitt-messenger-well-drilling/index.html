01 — Introduction

A recovery partner for the jobs that got finished and never got paid

To the Hewitt-Messenger office — the hardest dollars a well and pump contractor collects are the ones where the water is already running. The rig has been on site, the casing and pump are in the ground, the crew has been paid, and the customer has what they called you for. From that point on, the only leverage left is persistence.

A past-due invoice isn’t a paperwork problem — it’s working capital you already spent. Rig hours, casing, a Franklin or Grundfos pump, fuel and payroll all went out the door before the money came back in. Every month a balance sits, contact information goes stale, memories of the job get fuzzier, and the odds of ever seeing it drop.

Southwest Recovery Services is an outsourced receivables recovery partner. We have worked commercial and consumer balances on pure contingency since 2004, from 12 offices across 7 states — Missouri included — with recovery reach in all 50 states. You place accounts through a secure portal that takes an aging export or a simple spreadsheet, and we work them. No recovery, no fee.

03 — Your questions, answered

The questions a family-run contractor actually asks

Starting with the one that matters most in a market where reputation travels faster than an invoice.

Your questionSouthwest Recovery Services
1. How our customers get treatedThis comes first because it should. You have 356 reviews at 4.5 stars in a market where everybody knows everybody, and one bad phone call costs more than the invoice. Every contact is compliant, documented and recorded — no threats, no misrepresentation. FDCPA, Reg F and TCPA guardrails are enforced inside the workflow, not left to one person’s judgment. Most people who owe a well contractor money are not refusing to pay; they are behind, embarrassed, or waiting on something. Treated that way, a large share of them pay.
2. Fee structurePure contingency — $0 upfront, no retainer, no monthly minimum, no setup cost. You pay a pre-agreed percentage only of what we actually recover. The rate is set by the age, size and mix of what you place; older and smaller balances carry a higher percentage because they take more work. We quote your rate once we have seen a representative sample of your aging.
3. Dry holes and water-yield disagreementsA dispute pauses recovery, it does not end it. Ozark karst means two wells a hundred feet apart can yield very differently, so “you didn’t find enough water” is a predictable objection on a job done correctly. We work it off the paper: the signed work authorization, the drilling log, depth and yield records, the DNR well certification, and the message trail. Where the documentation is clean, the objection usually collapses. Dispute investigation typically adds 30–60 days.
4. Customers who have gone quiet or moved onThe core of what we do. Persistent multi-channel outreach by phone, mail, email and text, plus skip tracing against Experian and Equifax data to locate a homeowner who sold the property, a tenant who left, or a business that closed and reopened under a new name. A third-party demand on our letterhead resolves a meaningful share of accounts that have stopped answering you.
5. Builder, commercial and agricultural accountsDifferent animal, same partner. On new construction the general contractor is often paid in full while your invoice waits, so we pursue the operating entity, its successors and its principals, and review legal escalation where an account still will not engage. Farm and irrigation balances get worked around the season, not against it.
6. Onboarding and placementLow-lift. Bulk-upload an aging export from QuickBooks, ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro or a plain spreadsheet through the portal — we map your columns, so nothing gets re-keyed. Outreach begins within 24–48 hours of a documented placement, and you keep live visibility on every account from there.

The age curve

Why the date you place matters more than the rate you pay

Nothing moves recovery on a service-trade book as much as age. A homeowner who still remembers the crew in the driveway engages very differently from one two years removed from the job, at a new address, with a phone number that no longer works.

What age does

Balances under roughly 90 days past due often resolve inside 30–60 days. The same invoice at eighteen months collects a fraction of that — the gap between the 59% and 8% cases above is mostly age and documentation, not effort.

The standing rule

A fixed “anything past 90 days goes out” policy recovers materially more than letting balances drift toward write-off. On pure contingency there is no cost to placing early, so the only thing waiting buys you is a smaller check.

Recovery varies by portfolio. Before any account is worked, SWRS gives you an honest, age-based read on a representative sample of your file — including the parts we think will be hard.
