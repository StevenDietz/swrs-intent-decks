03 — Your questions, answered

The questions a family business actually asks

You told us balances run 31–60 days out and that you are looking at third-party collections. Here is how that works in practice.

Your questionSouthwest Recovery Services
1. Fee structurePure contingency — $0 upfront, no retainer, no monthly minimum. You pay a pre-agreed percentage only of what we actually recover. Your 31–60 day aging earns a better rate than an old book; fresh balances are the cheapest and most recoverable accounts we work.
2. How your customers are treatedThis matters more in your business than in most. Your buyers post reviews, join breed groups and refer their friends. Every contact is calm, compliant and documented — no threats, no misrepresentation, no pressure. A recovery partner who embarrasses a customer has cost you more than the balance.
3. Deposits, balances and payment plansThree different problems. A forfeited deposit turns on what your contract says; a pickup-day balance is usually a simple collection; a defaulted payment plan needs structured follow-up over months. We work all three, and the written agreement decides how hard each one can be pushed.
4. Disputed accountsA dispute pauses recovery, it does not end it. We validate against the paper — your purchase agreement, health guarantee, deposit terms, the message history — and speak with both sides. Clear signed terms usually resolve it inside 30–60 days.
ComplianceConsumer balances fall under the FDCPA and Reg F, and we work them inside those rules by design. That protects you as much as the customer: a business that hands accounts to an aggressive collector inherits the liability.

Why 31–60 days is the best position to place from

The same balance is worth several times more now than a year from now

Your webform says 31–60 days. That is the most useful number you gave us, because recovery tracks the age of a balance more closely than any other variable — the 92% and the 20% above are the same kind of account at different ages.

The age curve

Fresh balances resolve at multiples of what a one- or two-year-old account returns; contact data is still good and the purchase is still recent in the customer’s mind.

The standing rule

A fixed “place at 60 days” policy recovers materially more than letting balances drift — and on contingency there is no cost to placing.

Recovery figures vary by portfolio; SWRS gives an honest, age-based read on a representative sample before any accounts are worked.
