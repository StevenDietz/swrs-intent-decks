03 — Your questions, answered

The questions an integrator actually asks — answered directly

You reached out about a single unpaid B2B invoice. Here is exactly how third-party recovery works for project-based engineering AR, in the order it matters.

Your questionSouthwest Recovery Services
1. Fee structurePure contingency — $0 upfront, no retainer, no monthly minimum, no per-account fee. You pay a pre-agreed percentage only of what we actually recover. Commercial rates scale with the age, balance and volume of what you place; a single invoice and a full aging file are quoted differently, and we tell you which makes sense for you.
2. Disputes over scope or performanceA scope or performance dispute pauses recovery — it doesn’t end it. We validate the balance against the paper trail: the signed proposal or PO, the change-order authorizations, FAT and commissioning sign-offs, and the correspondence record. In integration work, a signed acceptance document is usually decisive. Dispute investigation typically adds 30–60 days.
3. Owners and EPCs that go silentThe core of what we do. Persistent multi-channel outreach, commercial skip tracing to locate the operating entity, successors and principals behind a business that has gone quiet, and legal-escalation review for accounts that still will not engage. A third-party demand on agency letterhead alone resolves a meaningful share of silent accounts.
4. Held retainage and stalled milestonesLarge plant owners and EPCs rarely refuse to pay — they stall inside procurement. We work AP directly: matching invoice to PO and receipt, chasing the approval that stalled, and escalating to the buyer of record with documented, compliant outreach rather than another project-manager voicemail.
5. Onboarding & placementFast and low-lift. Place a single invoice, or bulk-upload an AR aging export through the portal — we map your columns, so no one re-keys anything. Outreach begins within 24–48 hours of a documented placement, and your engineers stay on billable work.

The age curve

Why a fresh project invoice recovers best

A commercial balance is most collectible while it is fresh; every month it ages, contact data goes stale, the engineer or PM who authorized the work moves on, and leverage erodes. The highest-leverage change most engineering firms make is not chasing harder — it is placing sooner.

Fresh vs. aged

Accounts under roughly 90 days past due often resolve in 30–60 days; balances that sit a year collect a fraction of that — which is what the 33–34% cards above look like.

The standing rule

A fixed “place at 90 days” policy recovers materially more than letting balances drift toward write-off — and on contingency there is no cost to placing.

Recovery figures vary by portfolio; SWRS gives an honest, age-based read on a representative sample before any accounts are worked.

05 — Built for engineering & project receivables

We speak DSO — and we reduce it

Every past-due project invoice is a DSO problem before it is a collections problem. The Hackett Group puts median DSO at 43.5 days, while the top quartile collects in 25.1 — those firms touch their cash more than 18 days sooner. When we recover an aged balance it comes off your receivables the day it clears, which moves the metric directly. The 60-day escalation rule

Internal follow-up handles 15–30 days past due and management takes 30–60. Past 60, a documented third-party escalation recovers materially more than another PM voicemail — and on contingency it costs nothing to place.
