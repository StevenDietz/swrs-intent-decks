Thank you for a genuinely well-built inquiry — your twelve items are the right twelve, and they told me more about your portfolio than most discovery calls do. This portfolio fits us squarely. Answers in your order, then times to meet.

Residential HVAC and plumbing experience. This is an active vertical for us, not an adjacency. Our current book includes residential HVAC, plumbing, roofing, pool, electrical and irrigation contractors, several of them multi-market Texas operators placing the same $99-to-$20,000 spread you described.

Recovery rates and timelines. Age at placement drives recovery more than any other single factor. Accounts under roughly 90 days past due commonly resolve in 30 to 60 days; balances that have sat a year or more recover a fraction of that. The capabilities overview below shows real placed-versus-recovered results from our home-services book, and I have deliberately left the weak ones in — the same page carries an 83% result and a 6% result, and the difference is almost entirely how old the accounts were on arrival. I would rather you see that now than discover it after you place.

Contingency fees and additional costs. The full schedule is attached. Pure contingency, $0 upfront, no retainer, no monthly minimum, nothing owed on anything we do not collect. Credit bureau reporting, the client portal, reporting, remittance and pre-collection letters are all included at no additional cost for a portfolio of your type. The only costs that ever sit outside contingency are court, filing and recording fees on accounts you specifically authorize for legal or lien action — quoted per account, in advance, and never incurred without your written approval.

Small and large balances. One process, one rate, no balance floor. We do not cherry-pick a file and hand the small accounts back.

Pre-collection and early intervention. Our Early-Out program works accounts in the roughly 15-to-60-day window before they reach full collections — address validation and NCOA scrub, a structured notice series, professional outbound contact, then accounts returned or escalated at your direction. For most home-services books this is where the margin is.

Payment plans and settlement authority. Both are included and both operate inside authority you set at onboarding. Anything outside the limits you give us comes back to you for approval.

Credit bureau reporting. Offered, and included at no setup fee for your portfolio. We report and update qualifying consumer accounts to Experian and Equifax under FCRA and Metro 2.

Texas mechanic’s liens — a candid answer. Lien-eligible accounts are identified at placement and routed to our filing partner or counsel, with costs advanced by you and quoted per account. But you should hear the limitation from me now rather than from a lawyer later: Texas allows a lien against a homestead only where a written contract was signed before work began, by both spouses, and recorded. Most routine residential service invoices will not clear that bar. Where lien rights are genuinely live — commercial jobs, non-homestead property, properly papered replacements — we pursue them. For everything else, credit reporting and statutory interest are the real levers, and we will tell you at placement which of your accounts fall where.

I have also attached a detailed capability brief on Texas mechanic’s liens for your book specifically — the statutory deadline table for original-contractor and derivative claims, the homestead rules in full, the enforcement routes once an affidavit is filed, and what we do instead on the accounts where lien rights are not available. If liens are a real part of your evaluation, that brief will answer more than a call can.

Legal escalation. Nothing goes to an attorney without your written approval and a per-account quote showing the attorney’s contingency rate and the itemized court and filing costs. You decide whether to proceed.

Reporting, portal and placement workflow. Everything runs through a secure client portal: upload an aging export from ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, QuickBooks or whatever you run — we map your columns, so there is no re-keying — then track account status, run reports on demand, exchange work orders and invoices, log any payment a homeowner makes directly to you, and pull monthly remittance statements.
