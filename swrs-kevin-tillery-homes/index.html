03 — Your questions, answered

The questions a rental owner actually asks

You told us you are carrying $10,000–$100,000 in outstanding balances across a rental portfolio. Here is exactly how that gets worked.

Your questionSouthwest Recovery Services
1. Fee structurePure contingency — $0 upfront, no retainer, no monthly minimum. You pay a pre-agreed percentage only of what we actually recover. On a book your size that means the write-offs you are already carrying cost you nothing to attempt.
2. Tenants who have moved onThe core of rental recovery. Skip tracing locates a former tenant who has changed address, phone and employer — the single biggest reason owners give up on these balances. A national reach matters here; a tenant who left Akron rarely stays in Akron.
3. Credit reportingThis is the leverage most owners do not have on their own. Reportable balances go to Equifax and Experian, including Experian RentBureau, which is specific to rental history and is checked by the next landlord screening that tenant. It is often what turns an ignored balance into a paid one.
4. Unpaid rent vs. damagesTwo different arguments. Unpaid rent is documented by the lease and the ledger; damages need the move-out inspection, photographs and itemized costs. We work both, and the strength of your file decides how hard each can be pushed — which is why placement documentation matters so much.
ComplianceRental balances are consumer debt under the FDCPA and Reg F, and we work them inside those rules by design. That protects you: an owner who hands accounts to an aggressive collector inherits the liability. Note we do not work consumer accounts in CA, OR or WA; Minnesota is restricted.

Why file quality decides a vacated balance

The lease and the move-out file are worth more than the chase

The gap between the 100% and the 19% above is rarely effort — it is documentation and timing. A balance placed soon after move-out, with a signed lease, a clean ledger and an itemized damage file, is a fundamentally different account from one placed two years later on a handshake.

What makes a balance collectable

Signed lease, itemized ledger, move-out inspection with dated photographs, forwarding address and employer if known — each one raises the recoverable share.

The standing rule

Place at move-out plus 30–60 days rather than at write-off. Contact data is still warm and the tenant has not yet re-established elsewhere.

Recovery figures vary by portfolio; SWRS gives an honest, age-based read on a representative sample before any accounts are worked.

Damages beyond the deposit
