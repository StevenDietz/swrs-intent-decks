The aging curve

Why 90+ behaves differently — and what still moves

A trade receivable is most collectable while the job is fresh, the technician’s notes are current and the person who approved the work is still in the chair. Past 90 days, contact data goes stale and the account competes with every other bill on the customer’s desk. That is the book you are describing, and it is still worth placing — on contingency it costs nothing to try.

What recovers best in your file

Accounts with a signed work order or approved proposal, an itemized invoice, and a named approver. Documentation is the single biggest predictor in the trades.

What the next book should do

A standing rule to place at 90 days recovers materially more than letting balances drift toward write-off, and it stops the next $155K from aging the same way.

Recovery figures vary by portfolio; SWRS gives an honest, age-banded read on a representative sample before any accounts are worked. No recovery is guaranteed.
