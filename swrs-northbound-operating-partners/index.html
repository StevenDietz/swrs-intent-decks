01 — Introduction

When the claim was paid and the contractor still wasn’t

To the NorthBound case management team — the hardest dollars a restoration company collects aren’t disputed invoices. They’re the jobs where the loss was documented, the carrier issued payment, and the money landed somewhere other than the contractor who did the work.

Two very different files sit inside one aging report. A property owner who received the carrier’s check and never endorsed it over is a collectable receivable. Proceeds sitting with a public adjuster, a law firm or another third party is a fund-diversion question — it moves under demand sometimes, and needs counsel the rest of the time. We will tell you which is which before anything is placed.

Southwest Recovery Services is an outsourced receivables recovery partner working commercial and consumer balances on pure contingency since 2004, licensed and operating in Florida with recovery reach in all 50 states. You place through a secure portal that takes your job-costing or AR-aging export. No recovery, no fee.

03 — Your questions, answered

Your six questions — answered in order

Exactly what you asked, plus the distinctions that decide what this portfolio actually recovers.

Your questionSouthwest Recovery Services
1. Commercial and consumer in FloridaBoth. SWRS is licensed and actively collecting in Florida on commercial B2B balances and on consumer accounts. The two are worked under different rules and by different teams — consumer files carry FDCPA, Regulation F and the Florida Consumer Collection Practices Act, commercial files do not. Segment them at placement and both recover better.
2. Public adjusters, law firms, contractors & third parties holding proceedsYes, with an honest line drawn. Where a third party holds funds owed to you, we identify and contact the holder, document the chain of payment from the carrier forward, and press for release. Where proceeds sit in an attorney trust account or with a licensed public adjuster who refuses, the remedy is legal — interpleader, a Florida DFS licensing complaint, a bar grievance, or suit. We will say so rather than bill you cycles against a file that needs counsel.
3. Carrier paid, you weren’tThis is the strongest part of your portfolio. Where the carrier issued payment to the insured and the insured never paid the contractor, the money demonstrably existed and the obligation is documented — that combination recovers materially better than an ordinary aged balance. Proof of carrier payment, the signed work authorization and the certificate of completion are what make these move.
4. FeesPure contingency — $0 upfront, no retainer, no monthly minimum. You pay a pre-agreed percentage only of what we actually recover. Skip tracing is included, not billed separately. Litigation is never filed without your written authorization on a file-by-file basis; court costs and any filing fees are quoted to you before suit and are the only out-of-pocket item in the relationship. We quote your rate once we’ve seen a representative sample of the aging.
5. Expected recoveryAge governs. At 12–24 months at placement this book sits toward the lower half of our restoration and contractor range, not the top of it — the case studies opposite show that spread honestly. The carrier-paid subset outperforms the rest of the file. We give an age-banded read on a representative sample before any account is worked, and we do not guarantee recovery.
6. Assignment & escalationBulk-upload an aging or job-costing export through the portal — we map your columns, no re-keying. Files are scored and assigned by balance, age and account type, with commercial and consumer routed separately. Outreach begins within 24–48 hours of a documented placement and escalates through multi-channel contact, skip tracing on the debtor and any third party holding funds, credit reporting where the account qualifies, and a recommendation for or against legal action — which only proceeds on your authorization.

The aging curve

Why a restoration balance decays the way it does

A restoration receivable is most collectable while the loss is fresh in everyone’s mind and the carrier file is still open. Once the claim closes, the adjuster moves on and the proceeds have been spent, leverage drops sharply — which is exactly what a 12–24 month book is describing.

What still moves at 12–24 months

Files where carrier payment is documented. The money existed, the obligation is provable, and that evidence survives aging better than an ordinary invoice does.

What the next book should do

A standing rule to place at 90–120 days past due recovers materially more than letting balances drift, and on contingency there is no cost to placing early.

Recovery figures vary by portfolio; SWRS gives an honest, age-banded read on a representative sample before any accounts are worked. No recovery is guaranteed.

05 — Built for restoration & property-services recovery

Following the money the claim already released

Restoration receivables fail in a small number of specific ways, and each one has a different answer. Sorting the file correctly at intake is most of the work.

Carrier paid, contractor unpaid

Documented proof of payment plus the signed work authorization turns a stale balance into a provable obligation.

Third parties holding proceeds

We identify and contact public adjusters, counsel and other holders, document the payment chain, and flag the files that need legal escalation rather than more outreach.

Commercial and consumer, separated

Two teams, two rulebooks. Florida consumer balances are worked under FDCPA, Regulation F and the FCCPA; commercial B2B is not consumer debt.

Multi-entity placement

Portfolio and family-office operators place several operating companies under one relationship with per-entity reporting.
