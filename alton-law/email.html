Upfront costs, first, since it is usually the deciding question. There are none. We work pure contingency and have since 2004: no retainer, no setup fee, no placement fee, no minimum, no monthly or annual fee, and no upfront legal expense. If nothing is recovered, your client is invoiced nothing — no clawback, no minimum draw, no cancellation charge. The fee comes only out of dollars actually collected, which means we carry the cost and the risk of working the file. Accounts can be recalled at any time in writing without penalty; the only carve-out is that contingency stands on dollars already recovered.

The process. An account is placed through a secure portal, and we validate the file before any outreach happens. From there it moves through a documented sequence: a written notice carrying the validation language the law requires, then coordinated letter, phone, email and SMS contact timed to when a debtor is most likely to engage. If someone has gone quiet, skip tracing through Experian data locates a current address, phone and employment. Where your client authorizes it and the account qualifies, balances can be reported to Equifax and Experian. Every contact is logged and auditable, which matters when counsel is standing behind the file.

Documentation to begin. The contract or signed agreement, the itemized invoice or statement, the payment ledger with the date of last payment, any change orders or approvals, the written communication trail, and any demand already sent. Then debtor identifiers: full legal name or the exact registered entity name, service and mailing address, phone and email. If the agreement carries an interest or late-fee provision, send that page — it may be recoverable. Thin files still get worked; complete files recover materially better.

Timelines. Outreach typically begins within 24 to 48 hours of a documented placement. Accounts that are still fresh, inside roughly ninety days of last payment, often resolve in thirty to sixty days. A balance that has sat a year recovers a fraction of that, which is the strongest argument I can make for placing sooner rather than later.

Other terms worth knowing. Settlements require your client’s authorization — either account by account, or inside a standing floor they set. Legal escalation is optional and never happens without written authorization; we are not a law firm, so a suit would run through a Texas-licensed attorney and any court costs are disclosed and authorized in advance. Placing an account with us closes no doors.

On the rate itself, I would rather be useful than fast. Our fee is a single stated percentage that goes on the agreement in writing before a single account is placed, and nothing else is ever billed. But that percentage is priced against the file, and I do not know the file yet. If I quote a number now it is a guess, and a guess is worth nothing to a client comparing real offers side by side.
