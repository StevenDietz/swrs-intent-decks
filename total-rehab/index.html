01 — Introduction

The balance that builds one visit at a time

To Taylor and the Total Rehab team — therapy is the one service line where a patient balance accrues visit by visit. A copay at every session, coinsurance on every unit, and a deductible that resets in January. Twenty visits into a course of care, a family that never saw a large bill is holding one.

You already do the hard part well. You verify benefits as a courtesy, bill the payer on the patient’s behalf, and collect only what the plan deems the patient’s responsibility. But as your own insurance page tells patients, a verification is not a guarantee of benefits — so the true patient balance often lands weeks after the visit, once the plan has adjudicated and the front desk has no more leverage.

Southwest Recovery Services is an outsourced receivables recovery partner working patient balances on pure contingency since 2004, with recovery reach across all 50 states. Aged patient accounts are placed through a secure portal that takes an export from your practice management system. No recovery, no fee.

Why age decides the outcome

The same balance is worth several times more at 90 days than at 24 months

Look again at the range above. The strong results came from balances placed while the phone number still worked and the patient still connected the bill to the care. The weak ones came in after two years of statements. Nothing about the collector changed — only the calendar did.

The age curve

Patient balances are highly collectable near the date of service and decay steadily from there — demographic data goes stale, families move, and the connection between the bill and the visit fades.

The standing rule

A fixed placement policy — after a set number of statement cycles, every month, without exception — recovers materially more than letting balances drift toward write-off. On contingency there is no cost to placing.

Recovery figures vary by portfolio; SWRS gives an honest, age-based read on a representative sample before any accounts are worked. We do not publish blended averages and we do not promise a recovery rate.
