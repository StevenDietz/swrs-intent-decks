In detail

Portfolio triage. Every one of the 36 accounts was measured individually against the four-year limitations period under Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §16.004, computed from that account's own date of service — producing a clean split between accounts still legally enforceable, accounts recoverable on a voluntary basis only, and accounts requiring action before imminent expiry.

Contact development and skip tracing. Every account without a working contact was researched. Eleven accounts now carry verified accounts-payable addresses where previously there was no deliverable contact. Named decision-makers were identified at the corporate debtors in place of generic mailboxes — on one account, both the accounts-payable manager who has owned that desk since the invoice was issued and the firm's chief financial officer, with verified email and direct phone for each. That work also established that the firm routes all payables through a centralized corporate function, which explains why years of correspondence directed to the local office never reached a payment decision-maker.

Entity and obligor verification. The largest debtor in the file was verified through the Texas Comptroller — active right to transact business, active Secretary of State registration, registered agent identified for service. On the hotel account the responsible management company was identified as the true obligor rather than the property. Franchise-versus-corporate obligor risk was flagged on three national-brand accounts, and each demand now requires the debtor to name the exact contracting entity or accept the party of record.

Record corrections. A duplicate account key carrying one balance a second time was confirmed removed, so that balance is never pursued or reported twice. One account was identified as having been referred to Axxon's own counsel six days after placement and has been withdrawn from our inventory. A date-of-service error recording one account three years later than the actual invoice date was corrected, and the remainder of the book is under review for the same error.

Demands and settlement. Five formal demand notices were issued. Each is drafted as a presentment of the claim under Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §38.002 — which starts the thirty-day statutory period after which reasonable attorney's fees become recoverable — establishes the account as an itemized account subject to suit on sworn account under Tex. R. Civ. P. 185, and states that statutory interest accrues under Tex. Fin. Code §302.002. Each carries a ten-business-day response requirement. A settlement offer was separately issued on the one account reaching limitations expiry on September 2.

6. Why Recovery Has Been Below Benchmark

This is the question the portfolio raises, and it deserves a direct answer rather than a defensive one.

Two structural causes — both correctable, neither of them collection effort

1. Age at placement. These accounts arrived an average of 2.7 years past due. Recovery probability on commercial receivables declines steeply and predictably with age: roughly 69% at three months, 21% at twelve months, under 9% at twenty-four. By the time an account reaches thirty months, the arithmetic has already decided most of the outcome. Two accounts in this file reached our office with the legal remedy itself already expired or within days of expiring.

2. Contact quality at placement. The contacts supplied were, in most cases, job-site technician contacts rather than accounts-payable contacts. A significant share of the 1,530 logged actions was therefore directed at people with no authority to authorize payment. The clearest example: on the largest account in the file, correspondence went for roughly a year to a property reservations inbox. Once the correct accounts-payable contact was identified today, that account moved to formal demand the same day.

What that means practically

Neither cause is fixed by working the accounts harder, and neither can be repaired retroactively on the balances already placed. Both are fixed at intake, and both are addressed in items 7 and 8 of Section 5. The accounts placed under the corrected process should be expected to perform very differently from this file.
