1. Where we went wrong

  1. We accepted thin files and ran them anyway. Each placement came with one phone number, no ID and no second contact. We loaded them without telling you that was not enough to work with.
  2. We ran automation instead of working accounts. Of 17,911 notes on your accounts, 136 were written by a person. 20 accounts have no human note at all, and the typical last human note is about three months old. The automation sent 5,839 emails and made 5,024 calls, and almost nothing came back.
  3. We kept contacting addresses you had told us were bad. Your files flagged bounced emails and delivery-site addresses. We kept using them.
  4. We never asked you for what we needed. Everything we sent you was a thank-you or a missing-field notice. We never asked about agreements, signers, card on file or where the containers are.
  5. We made your documents hard to use. Your files sit on your client account, not on each debtor. Each monthly invoice became its own account, so one debtor can have five accounts, each with its own letters and emails. Our acknowledgments of your uploads were slow, with a median of three days and some far longer.

2. What we need from MI-BOX to succeed, and why each one matters

We needWhy it matters to collecting
Signed agreement for each debtor, and who signed itIt is the proof of what the debtor agreed to: rent, late charges, the card authorization. Without it, one dispute can stop an account. On business accounts it also shows who signed and whether a person, not just the company, is responsible.
Second phone, alternate contact, working emailWe received one phone number per debtor. When it fails, we have no other way to reach them. 23 of your 180 placement rows say the email bounces.
Billing address, apart from the delivery addressLetters and notices sent to a job site or a delivery location never reach the debtor. Your files flagged 13 of these. We cannot locate or confirm who we are dealing with from a delivery address.
Card on file: declined or not, and a retryThe agreement lets MI-BOX charge the card after 30 days late. It is the fastest, cheapest recovery. If it fails, it tells us what to expect. 39 of the invoices we have show a declined card.
Container status: on site, picked up, dateShows whether MI-BOX still holds the container and its contents, which is real leverage, and whether rent is still adding to the balance.
Damage evidence: photos, work orders, repair costDamage charges are the first thing debtors dispute ($10,164 on 17 one-time invoices). Evidence is what resolves a dispute and lets us keep working the account.
Payments received directly, and payment plansSo we never contact someone who has paid or is on a plan, and the balance we quote is right.
Short summary on your 15 largest accountsThe history that does not fit in a file: promises, conversations, disputes. A person can start at the right place, not from zero. We only have a summary for 5 of your 63 debtors.
One monthly file per debtor, with a ledgerSeparates rent, late fees and damages so a balance can be explained and defended, and puts one debtor on one account.
Identifiers, whatever you have: date of birth, last four of driver’s license or SSN, business tax ID, employerThese let us confirm we have the right person, find them when they move, check bankruptcy, deceased and military status before we contact anyone, and match the account for credit reporting. None of the 180 placement rows had a date of birth or SSN. Your agreement uses the last four of the driver’s license as the access PIN, so you may already have it.
Why speed matters. Your accounts reached us a median of about 140 days after the invoice was due, and 43 of the 111 invoices we have were over 180 days old. Industry benchmark data (Commercial Collection Agencies of America) puts the chance of collecting at about 69% at three months past due, 51% at six, 21% at twelve and 9% at twenty-four. Those are benchmarks, not a promise, but the direction is clear: each month of waiting costs recovery, and missing contact details and identifiers make every one of those months worse.
