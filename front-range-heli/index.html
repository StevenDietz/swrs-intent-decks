03 — Your questions, answered

Your questions — answered directly

Process, conduct, and rates, in the order you asked them, plus the specifics that matter for a book of flight-training balances between $500 and $25,500.

Your questionSouthwest Recovery Services
1. How your former students will be treatedThis is the first thing we scope, not the last. Every account is worked with respectful, documented, empathy-first contact — no threats, no misrepresentation, no pressure tactics, no contact outside legal hours. Our founding standard is that recovery must preserve goodwill and represent the client professionally, and our conduct is auditable account by account. You can review the outreach we plan to use before a single account is worked.
2. Our recovery processPlacement, then validation, then structured outreach. We confirm the balance against your paperwork (training agreement, block-time purchase, invoices, the communication trail), send the required validation notice, then work a coordinated email, mail, SMS and voice sequence timed to when a person is most likely to engage. Where someone has moved or gone quiet, skip tracing locates current contact data. Most of what resolves does so in the first 30–90 days.
3. Fee structurePure contingency — $0 upfront, no retainer, no monthly minimum. You pay a pre-agreed percentage only of what we actually recover. The rate is driven by the age, balance and volume of what you place; we quote it once we have seen a representative sample of your aging, so the number you get is real rather than a brochure figure.
4. Your $500 to $25,500 rangeThat spread is normal for flight training and it does not need to be split. Smaller balances respond well to volume-efficient digital outreach; the larger ones — a lapsed block-time purchase or an unfinished commercial rating — justify individual handling, negotiated payment arrangements, and where warranted a review of legal escalation. Same portal, same contingency, different intensity.
5. Disputes and students still on good termsA dispute pauses recovery; it does not end it. We work it against the paper and speak with both sides. If a former student wants to return and finish the rating, tell us — we will structure the resolution so that the door stays open rather than closing it. That is usually the better commercial outcome for a school your size.
GeographyRecovery reach across all 50 states, which matters because flight students relocate — for work, for a job with a regional, or back home after training. A balance does not become uncollectible because the person left Colorado.

What actually moves the number

Age at placement, not pressure

The spread above is not mostly about effort. It tracks how old the balances were when they were placed. The aviation book at the top was placed while the accounts were still relatively fresh; the 14% book at the bottom was placed after a long in-house collection effort had already exhausted the contact data and the goodwill.

The age curve

Balances under roughly 90 days past due often resolve within 30–60 days. The same balance at 18 months recovers a fraction of that — the phone number is dead, the address is stale, and the person has stopped expecting to hear from you.

The standing rule

A fixed “place at 90 days” policy recovers materially more than letting balances drift toward write-off, and on pure contingency there is no cost to placing early.

Recovery varies by portfolio. SWRS gives an honest, age-based read on a representative sample before any account is worked — including telling you when a balance is not worth pursuing.

05 — Built for flight training & aviation services

Protecting the rating you earned from your customers

A five-star reputation is a commercial asset, and it is the reason most flight schools write off money they are owed rather than risk a public complaint. It is a false choice. Firm, compliant, documented recovery and a protected brand are the same discipline, not competing ones.

Unfinished training & lapsed block time

Prepaid hours drawn down and never settled, and students who stop flying mid-rating with a balance outstanding — the two accounts that make up most of a flight school’s write-offs.

Students who have moved on

Skip tracing locates former students who have relocated for a regional job or gone home after training, so distance stops being the reason a balance dies.

Commercial and corporate accounts

Charter, aerial-survey, photography and corporate-event invoices are commercial B2B receivables and are worked as such — one partner, one portal, both sides of your book.

Compliance you can point to

FDCPA, Reg F and TCPA guardrails enforced inside the workflow with consent and number checks before contact, documented on every account — the record you need if a former student ever complains.
