03 — Your questions, answered

Your eight questions — answered directly, in order

Exactly what you asked, in the order you asked it. Everything below is confirmed in writing in the placement agreement before you place the first account.

Your questionSouthwest Recovery Services
1. Do you accept residential contractor accounts involving homeowner consumer debt?Yes. Residential and home-services contractors are an established segment for us — plumbing, HVAC, roofing, flooring, remodeling, garage doors, windows and general repair. Homeowner consumer balances are worked under full FDCPA, Regulation F and TCPA discipline, and under the Colorado Fair Debt Collection Practices Act. Colorado is an operating state for us. The only U.S. states where we do not take consumer placements are California, Oregon and Washington; Minnesota is restricted.
2. Contingency percentage — and any placement, setup, minimum or monthly fees?Pure contingency. There is no placement fee, no setup fee, no minimum, no monthly fee, no annual fee and no retainer. The contingency percentage is a single stated rate that appears on your agreement and is agreed in writing before you place a single account — it is set by the age, balance size and expected volume of what you place, and a fresh, fully documented ~$5,000 residential balance prices at the low end of the consumer range. Nothing else is ever billed to you.
3. Do you owe anything if no money is recovered?No. Nothing. If we recover $0, you are invoiced $0. That is the entire economic model — our fee comes only out of dollars actually collected, so we carry the cost and the risk of working the account. There is no clawback, no minimum draw and no cancellation charge.
4. How are direct payments from the customer handled?You report them to us the moment they land — there is a direct-payment reporting function in the client portal, or a phone call or email works. We update the balance immediately and stop contacting the homeowner for money already paid. Be aware of the one thing every honest agency will tell you up front: the agreed contingency percentage does apply to payments a homeowner makes directly to you on an account that is already placed, because the placement is what produced the payment. It does not apply to anything collected before placement, and it does not apply to accounts you never place.
5. Do settlements require your approval?Yes. No balance is compromised without your authorization. The default is per-account written approval — we bring you the offer, you accept or decline. If you would rather not be asked every time, you can grant standing authority at a floor you choose (for example, “accept anything at or above 90% of principal”) and we work inside it. Either way, the authority is yours and it is documented on the file.
6. Is legal escalation optional, and does it require your written authorization?Optional, and never without your written authorization. No account is ever referred for suit automatically. If an account looks legally viable, we bring you a recommendation with the facts — balance, documentation strength, asset and employment indicators, statute of limitations — and you decide. Southwest Recovery Services is not a law firm; suit is handled through a network attorney licensed in Colorado, and any court costs are disclosed and authorized by you in advance. You are never obligated to litigate, and declining costs you nothing.
7. Does recalling an account create a fee or penalty?No recall fee, no penalty, no notice period. You can pull any account back at any time, for any reason, in writing. The one carve-out is the obvious one: if money was already recovered, or a payment arrangement we secured is already performing, the contingency on those recovered dollars stands. Withdrawing a file that has produced nothing costs you nothing.
8. What documentation do you need to review and place the first account?For a residential contractor account, the file that recovers is: the signed customer agreement or contract, the estimate, every approved change order with the approval record, the final itemized invoice, the payment ledger and date of last payment, the completion documentation and photographs, material receipts, the written communication trail, and any prior demand you sent. Plus debtor identifiers: full legal name, service address, current mailing address, phone, email. If the contract carries an interest or late-fee provision, send that page — it may be recoverable. You already keep all of this, which is exactly why your file is stronger than most.
On disputed invoices and post-completion complaintsThis is the heart of contractor recovery and it deserves its own answer. A homeowner raising a concern after the work is done is not automatically a dispute — and a genuine dispute pauses recovery, it does not end it. We validate against the paper: signed agreement, approved change orders, completion photos, the message trail. In our experience most post-completion objections resolve on documentation alone. Where a dispute is real, we tell you honestly, stop pressing, and hand the account back rather than manufacture pressure that would expose you.
