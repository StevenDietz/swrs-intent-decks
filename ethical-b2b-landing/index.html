The cost of the wrong agency

Hiring the wrong collection agency doesn’t just fail to recover your money. It can cost you the customer, your reputation, and a complaint with your company’s name on it.

Pressure tactics end recoverable relationships

Most past-due business customers are not refusing to pay. They are disputing a line item, short on cash, or have simply lost the invoice. Threats and relentless calls turn a solvable problem into a lost account.

Their conduct is attributed to you

The agency speaks in your name. Complaints, bad reviews and demand letters triggered by aggressive collection tend to land on the company that placed the account — not only on the agency.

Your reputation travels faster than your invoice

Business owners talk, and one aggressive call to a controller can reach your other customers. How a debt is collected is part of how your company is judged.

Why the hard approach fails in B2B

In commercial collections, the person on the other end is rarely a lone individual. It is an accounts-payable clerk, a controller or an owner — someone who may be your customer next quarter. Business-to-business invoices usually involve a contract, a purchase order and an ongoing commercial relationship, which means the dispute, if there is one, is often about scope, delivery or a billing error rather than unwillingness to pay.

The federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act covers consumer debts — obligations arising from transactions primarily for personal, family or household purposes (15 U.S.C. § 1692a(5)). A business invoice generally falls outside it. That does not make every tactic acceptable: state law, the TCPA and plain commercial reputation still apply. It does mean conduct standards are set by the agency you hire. Ours are the same on every account.

Timing

The cost of waiting: collectability falls every month

The most controllable factor in how much of an overdue invoice you recover is when you place it. Commercial Collection Agencies of America data, republished by the Credit Research Foundation, measures the probability of collecting a receivable by how long it has been past due:

1 month past due88.7%
3 months past due68.9%
6 months past due51.3%
9 months past due37.5%
12 months past due21.4%
18 months past due15.2%
24 months past due8.9%

What that means in dollars (illustrative). On a $100,000 receivable, the table implies roughly $88,700 of expected recovery at one month past due, about $51,300 at six months and about $21,400 at twelve months. The same invoice, placed six months earlier, is worth roughly $37,000 more in expected value. Treat this as an illustration of the curve, not a guarantee for any single account.

Source: Commercial Collection Agencies of America (CCAoA) data republished by the Credit Research Foundation, “Benchmarking the Collectability of Your Accounts Receivable.” Recovery varies by account, debtor and economy.

That is why we recommend placing an account as soon as your own follow-up has stalled, rather than after several rounds of internal reminders. Placing early also keeps the contact details, the paper trail and the debtor’s memory of the transaction fresh.

What to send with a commercial account

A complete file lets us start contact the same day and gives us what we need if an account is disputed. For a business-to-business account, gather:

  • Legal business nameEntity name, trade names and, if known, state of formation.
  • AddressesBilling address, service or delivery address and any known prior addresses.
  • Accounts-payable contactsNames, titles, phone numbers and emails for AP, the controller and the owner.
  • Itemized invoicesInvoice numbers, dates, amounts, payments and credits.
  • Signed agreementContract, credit application or terms of service.
  • Proof of delivery or servicePurchase orders, delivery confirmations and work orders.
  • Payment historyLast payment date and amount, and any promised payment dates.
  • Correspondence and disputesEmails, notes and any dispute history.
  • Guarantor detailsPersonal guarantee and guarantor contact information, if one exists.
  • Anything you haveMissing items do not stop us. Send what you have and we will work with it.
