01 Consumer ICP Snapshot — The Fundamental Difference from B2B
Strategic Framing Consumer collections is NOT a smaller version of B2B. The entire model flips: volume replaces balance size as the yield driver, compliance complexity multiplies (FDCPA, CFPB Regulation F, TCPA, state overlays), and the right client relationship lasts years with thousands of placements — not one or two invoices. The ICP here is built around volume thresholds, vertical compliance fit, and unit yield floors, not individual account balance.

The Volume-Balance Matrix The real consumer ICP is defined by two axes: volume and average balance. High volume + high balance = best case (ClubCorp, KLLM). High volume + low balance = viable only at extreme scale (utilities). Low volume + high balance = look again — this is probably a B2B account, not consumer. Low volume + low balance = hard pass every time.
Freshness is Non-Negotiable Consumer accounts age out faster than B2B. The collectability decay curve hits harder on consumer paper — right-party contact (RPC) data degrades quickly, debtors move, change phones. A consumer client placing 12-month-aged debt will have half the yield of one placing 60-day-aged debt. Always ask: when was it last worked? When was it originated?

  • Average account balance $500+ (sweet spot $1,000–$3,500)
  • Debt originated in the last 12 months — not a portfolio dump
  • Clean consumer documentation: signed agreements, itemized bills, enrollment forms
  • Operating in SWRS-licensed consumer states (not CA, WA, OR)
  • HIPAA BAA-ready (healthcare clients) — willing to execute before placement
  • Has tried internal collections or prior agency with unsatisfactory results
  • Service-rendered debt (not predatory lending or payday loans)
  • Multi-location or regional operator — not a single-unit micro-business
  • Comfortable with Regulation F compliance (digital notices, itemization)
  • Decision maker is AR director, CFO, or revenue cycle manager — has authority
✗ Disqualified — Do Not Pursue
  • Consumer debt in California, Washington, or Oregon — hard stop, no exceptions
  • Under 100 placements per year with no scale growth path
  • Average balance under $200/account
  • Debt is older than 24 months with no prior collection action
  • No signed documentation — verbal agreements, informal billing
  • Predatory lending, payday loans, high-interest consumer lending — compliance risk
  • Single-location micro-business (solo practitioner, small daycare, single gym)
  • Healthcare client unwilling to execute HIPAA BAA
  • Wants to place consumer debt as a one-time portfolio dump only
  • Prior regulatory action (CFPB consent order, state AG complaint) on their portfolio
  • Accounts already in bankruptcy proceedings
06 Consumer Compliance Framework — SWRS Differentiators
Compliance IS the Sales Pitch for Consumer In consumer collections, compliance competence is not a checkbox — it is the primary reason clients choose SWRS over a cheaper competitor. Every vertical below has a specific compliance overlay. A prospect that doesn't ask about compliance in the first conversation is a red flag, not a green light.
FDCPA + CFPB Regulation F
Applies to all consumer collections. Regulation F (2021) adds:
  • Electronic communication opt-in/opt-out rules
  • Itemization date requirement on validation notices
  • 7-in-7 call frequency limit per debtor per week
  • Social media contact restrictions
  • Model validation notice compliance
SWRS differentiator: all collectors trained on Regulation F; model notices implemented.
HIPAA (Healthcare Clients)
Required for all medical, dental, behavioral health, hospital clients. SWRS obligations:
  • Business Associate Agreement (BAA) executed before first placement
  • PHI handled under HIPAA minimum necessary standard
  • Breach notification procedures in place
  • Collector training on PHI non-disclosure
  • Secure data transmission for placement files
SWRS differentiator: HIPAA-trained collectors; BAA standard in all healthcare contracts.
TCPA (Phone & Text)
Governs all phone and text contact with consumers. Key rules:
  • Prior express consent required for autodialed/prerecorded calls to cell phones
  • Do-Not-Call list scrubbing mandatory
  • Time-of-day restrictions (8am–9pm local debtor time)
  • Text opt-out honor required
  • Documented consent records required
SWRS differentiator: TCN + Skit.ai compliance layer; consent tracking built into workflow.
State-Level Overlays
Licensed in 47 states for consumer. Key state overlays:
  • TX: TDCA licensing + biennial renewal
  • FL: OFR licensure requirements
  • CO: CO FDCPA state overlay (stronger than federal)
  • NY/NJ: NYC/NJ local rules if debtor resides there
  • MN: Restricted placement — verify before accepting
  • CA, WA, OR: NO consumer collections — hard stop
Experian RentBureau (PM Vertical)
Property management exclusive differentiator:
  • Unpaid rent reported directly to Experian RentBureau
  • Appears on tenant screening reports — not just standard credit pulls
  • Creates payment incentive without litigation
  • FCRA compliance required for all reported balances
  • Dispute handling under FCRA Section 623
Competitors who cannot report to RentBureau lose on this vertical.
FCRA (Credit Reporting)
Applies when SWRS reports consumer accounts to credit bureaus:
  • Data furnisher obligations under FCRA Section 623
  • Dispute investigation and response within 30 days
  • Accuracy requirements on all reported tradelines
  • Experian data furnisher relationship maintained
  • Medical debt reporting rules (CFPB 2024 rulemaking — track for changes)
07 Decision Maker Personas — Consumer Side
Primary Buyer
Revenue Cycle Director
Profile

Healthcare, hospital, or large consumer services operator. Owns the full AR cycle from billing through collections. Evaluates agencies on compliance track record, recovery rate, and patient/member experience impact. Reports to CFO.

Pain Points
  • Prior agency had TCPA/FDCPA complaints that created liability
  • Collection rates declining; aged bucket growing
  • No visibility into what agency is doing or saying to patients/members
  • Need HIPAA BAA and audit trail
Trigger

"We had a compliance incident with our last vendor. We need someone we can trust."

Economic Buyer
CFO / VP Finance
Profile

Multi-location consumer operator (gym chain, PM company, driving school). Makes the vendor decision based on recovery rate, fee structure, and compliance risk. Compares SWRS to 2–3 other agencies. Contingency model resonates immediately.

Pain Points
  • Writing off 8–12% of annual revenue to bad debt
  • Prior agency fees felt high for results delivered
  • Board/ownership asking why collections aren't higher
  • No benchmarking data on what's achievable
Trigger

"We're writing off $2M a year. I know we should be recovering more of that."

Day-to-Day Contact
AR Manager / Office Manager
Profile

Handles the actual placement workflow. Manages file uploads, monitors recovery reports, fields debtor callbacks escalated from SWRS. Evaluates the agency on ease of use, communication, and portal functionality. Will champion SWRS internally if the experience is smooth.

Pain Points
  • Prior agency required complex file formats with no help
  • Couldn't get status updates on specific accounts
  • Angry patients/members called the practice about collection calls
  • Manual monthly reporting with no portal access
Trigger

"I just need a system that works and doesn't create more work for me."
