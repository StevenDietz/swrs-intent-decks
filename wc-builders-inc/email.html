I’m Steven Dietz, CEO of Southwest Recovery Services. We’re an outsourced receivables recovery partner, and I’m writing about the part of a remodeling job that rarely goes to plan: the last payment. A short overview built for WC Builders is below, and I’ll keep this email to the straight answers.

What it costs. Pure contingency — $0 upfront, no retainer, no minimum. You pay a pre-agreed percentage of what we actually recover, and nothing if we recover nothing. I quote the rate once I’ve seen a representative sample of your aging, not before.

How we work an account. Every account is scored first to set the contact strategy, then worked with compliant, multi-channel outreach — email, live and AI-assisted calls, voicemail and mailed notices — timed to when that customer is likely to engage. If there is no reply, the workflow escalates to locating the customer or a legal review with counsel. Homeowners are treated with patience and dignity; most of these balances don’t need pressure, they need a clear statement of what is owed and an easy way to pay. Everything runs through a secure 24/7 client portal, and every contact is documented under FDCPA, Reg F and TCPA rules.

An honest note on the numbers. We have no deep book of residential remodelers, so the case studies in the overview come from comparable trades and contractors — roofing, plumbing, HVAC, electrical — at a similar scale. They run from 88% recovered down to 16%, and I included the low end on purpose. Balances recover best while they are fresh; the older they get, the lower the number.

What we’d need to start. The signed contract and any change orders, the invoice or final-payment statement, photos or inspection sign-offs, the text or email trail, and the customer’s contact details, uploaded through the client portal. If your jobs run on progress billing, a simple invoice list is enough to begin. We also offer AR management for ongoing balances, if that is ever useful.
