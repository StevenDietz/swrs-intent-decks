03 — Your questions, answered

The questions a remodeler asks first — answered directly

What an owner-run contractor typically wants to know before placing a single account.

Your questionSouthwest Recovery Services
1. Fee structurePure contingency — $0 upfront, no retainer, no minimum. You pay a pre-agreed percentage of what we actually recover, and nothing if we recover nothing. We quote your rate once we’ve seen a representative sample of your aging.
2. Disputed balancesA dispute pauses recovery — it doesn’t end it. We validate the account against your paper: the signed contract, change orders, invoices, photos, inspection sign-offs and the text or email trail. Dispute investigation typically adds 30–60 days, and clean documentation is what separates a genuine dispute from a stall.
3. Homeowners who stop answeringThe core of what we do: persistent, respectful, multi-channel outreach to locate the customer and open a conversation. Most of these balances don’t need pressure — they need patience, a clear statement of what is owed, and an easy way to pay. Every contact follows FDCPA, Reg F and TCPA rules.
4. Liens and legal optionsLien deadlines are short and state-specific, so timing is a decision for your attorney. We flag accounts where timing may matter, and review legal escalation case by case with counsel for accounts that still won’t engage. Placing early keeps those options open.
5. Onboarding & placementLow-lift. Send an invoice list or an export from your accounting system or a simple spreadsheet through our portal; we map the columns, so there’s no re-keying. Outreach begins within 24–48 hours of a documented placement.

The timing advantage

Why a missed final payment recovers best when it’s fresh

A balance is most collectible while the project is still a recent memory. Every month it ages, contact details go stale and leverage erodes. The highest-leverage change most contractors make isn’t chasing harder — it’s placing sooner.

The age curve

Fresh accounts (under ~90 days past due) often resolve in 30–60 days; balances that sit a year collect a fraction of that.

The standing rule

A fixed “place at 90 days” policy recovers materially more than letting balances drift toward write-off — and on contingency there’s no cost to placing.

Recovery figures vary by portfolio; SWRS gives an honest, age-based read on a representative sample before any accounts are worked.
